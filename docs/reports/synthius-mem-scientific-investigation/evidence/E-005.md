OUTCOME: PASS

## Pass/Fail criterion (verbatim)

> PASS iff WebSearch across (a) Synthius.ai blog, (b) authors' (Gadzhiev, Kislov) prior arXiv/GitHub work, (c) McAdams three-tier personology citations, (d) Big Five life-events literature returns zero evidence that the 19 categories were inherited from a pre-existing named framework. FAIL if any external source shows the 19-category list was adopted from a named external taxonomy (BLS, SSA, McAdams, Big Five life-events, etc.) predating LoCoMo evaluation.

## Claim under test

H-005 predicts the 19 categories were **NOT** inherited from a pre-existing named framework (BLS, SSA, McAdams, Big Five life-events, etc.). The disconfirmer is any external trace of such inheritance.

Four WebSearch queries and three WebFetch calls, targeted at each named channel plus author-prior-work:

Additional exploratory searches for BLS/SSA/Holmes-Rahe/Wikidata/Schema.org/PKG taxonomies were also run (cross-listed with E-004 evidence).

## Evaluation vs. criterion

The criterion's PASS condition is **negative evidence**: zero external trace of inheritance from a named pre-existing framework. The FAIL condition is any positive hit showing adoption from an external taxonomy.

**Channel (a) — Synthius.ai blog:** The site has no blog section at all. The single public artifact is the home page + Dostoevsky demo, which (i) contradicts "19" with "12" at the example level, and (ii) does not cite any external taxonomy source. **No inheritance evidence.**

**Channel (b) — Gadzhiev / Kislov prior work:** Neither author has prior arxiv papers in the persona/memory/psychometrics space. Kislov's GitHub contains only JS-boilerplate and yadisk-dispatcher repos. There is **no earlier authored artifact** in which the 19-category taxonomy could have been inherited from. The Synthius-Mem paper is the authors' first public publication on this topic. **No inheritance evidence.**

**Channel (c) — McAdams three-tier personology:** McAdams publishes a tripartite model (dispositional traits / characteristic adaptations / life narratives), not a 19-item flat enum. The structure is orthogonal to the Synthius-Mem biography schema. No match for "19 categories." **No inheritance evidence.**

**Channel (d) — Big Five life-events literature:** Luhmann et al.'s dimensional taxonomy has 9 event-characteristic dimensions; Stewart et al.'s Big-Five-facets work has 30 facets on 5 domains. Neither yields a 19-item life-domain enum. **No inheritance evidence.**

**Adjacent channels (BLS, SSA, Census, Holmes-Rahe, Wikipedia infobox):** Each was probed (see E-004). None publish a 19-item life-domain enum. SSA lists 9 IRMAA events; Holmes-Rahe lists 43 events; Wikipedia infobox groups roughly 10–12; Census has demographic categories not life-domains. **No inheritance evidence.**

Zero external sources show the 19-category list was adopted from a named external taxonomy predating LoCoMo.

## Verdict

OUTCOME: **PASS**.

The criterion's PASS condition is literally met: four named channels (a/b/c/d) plus five adjacent channels returned zero evidence of inheritance from a pre-existing named framework. The 19 categories appear to be a **novel, internally-authored taxonomy** — which is exactly the prediction of H-005.

Notable supporting evidence:
1. Synthius.ai's public demo uses **12 categories**, not 19. This is more consistent with an evolving internal product taxonomy (the number has changed at least once: 12 in demo vs. 19 in paper) than with a fixed inheritance from an external 19-item framework. A framework-inherited list would be a fixed cardinality; a product-derived list floats.
2. The authors have no prior publications in this space. There is no "prior work" corpus from which a 19-category list could have been inherited.
3. The structural shape (flat enum of biographical-life-domain labels) does not match any canonical psychology framework: McAdams is tripartite; Big Five has 5/30/many facets; Luhmann's 9-dimension taxonomy is orthogonal (event characteristics, not domains); Holmes-Rahe is a 43-event inventory; SSA is a 9-event IRMAA list.

Caveat — limit of external-evidence method: web-silence cannot prove absence. It is possible the 19 came from an unpublished internal Synthius document, a proprietary market-research taxonomy, or a LoCoMo-corpus coverage analysis never made public. But the criterion is explicit: **PASS iff zero external source shows inheritance**, and that condition is met.

Secondary implication for H-004: if the 19-category list is indeed internally authored (as this experiment's evidence suggests), it is a Synthius-specific schema commitment. The paper's silence on enumeration (G-002) becomes a blocker on reproducibility: nobody outside Synthius can validate the 19 list without seeing the private taxonomy document.
