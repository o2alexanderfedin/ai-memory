### WebFetch 2: specific claim lookup on arXiv:2601.15313

**The paper does NOT contain the specific claim "RAG accuracy degrades from ~85% at 1K docs to ~45% at 10K docs".**

What it DOES contain (per WebFetch response, verbatim):
- "On Wikipedia facts, KO retrieval achieves 45.7% where Modern Hopfield Networks collapse to near-zero; hash-based retrieval maintains 100%."
- "scientific measurements (rho = 0.96, 0.02% accuracy at N=10,000)"
- "image embeddings (rho = 0.82, 0.05% at N=2,000)"

These are numerical findings about **Hopfield Networks / neural memory under semantic interference**, NOT about standard RAG performance. The mechanism (semantic interference) may be architecturally adjacent to RAG's vector-space crowding, but the specific 85%→45% 1K→10K claim in the Synthius-Mem paper (§2.1, §5.3) is **not substantiated by 2601.15313**.

## Reconciliation with paper under review

**Synthius-Mem paper §5.3 verbatim:** "At production scale with thousands of conversations, RAG performance would likely deteriorate further due to vector space crowding and semantic drift (arXiv:2601.15313)."

The arXiv:2601.15313 paper DOES support the general thesis of "embedding-space memory degrades with scale" (via semantic interference mechanism). But the numerical figures cited by Synthius-Mem ("~85% → ~45%") appear nowhere in 2601.15313.

### Possible explanations

1. **Mis-citation by Synthius-Mem authors**: They read the orthogonality-constraint paper and remembered its general claim about neural-memory degradation, but conflated or invented the specific percentage figures.
2. **Paraphrased but not quoted**: The cited paper may gesture at RAG-like systems with similar degradation patterns, and Synthius-Mem authors interpolated specific figures.
3. **Reading-assistant-generated fabrication**: The specific 85%/45% numbers may have been hallucinated during drafting (LLM-assisted writing).

None of these are consistent with a fully-defensible citation.
