## Metric analysis

### 1. Binary judge accuracy vs token-overlap F1

**Binary judge:** Given gold answer G and candidate C, a judge LLM emits 0 or 1 based on whether C is "correct" per rubric. Accuracy = (# correct) / (# total).

**Token-overlap F1:** Compute token-level precision/recall between G and C, harmonic mean:
- P = |tokens(G) ∩ tokens(C)| / |tokens(C)|
- R = |tokens(G) ∩ tokens(C)| / |tokens(G)|
- F1 = 2PR / (P+R)

These measure fundamentally different properties:
- Binary: semantic-correctness (can include paraphrases, different phrasing as "correct")
- F1: surface-level token overlap (penalizes paraphrases even if semantically correct)

### 2. §5.3 paper's own self-critique

Paper verbatim §5.3:
> "The original LoCoMo paper (Maharana et al., 2024) uses token-overlap F1. Mem0 (Singh et al., 2025) instructs its judge to 'be generous with grading.' Hippocampus uses a 5-point scale. ENGRAM's evaluation prompt is unpublished. Our binary rubric is described in Section 4.1 along with the reasoning for each level. To ensure cross-paper comparability we commonly use; results are in Section 4.2 and demonstrate that our lead is judge-robust."

The paper explicitly **acknowledges** the F1 vs binary disparity but **still performs the 94.37% vs 87.9% F1 comparison** in the abstract/§4.2 as if the numbers are directly comparable.

### 3. Three synthetic Q-A pairs demonstrating divergence

#### Pair 1 — Paraphrase (binary=1, F1 low)

**Question:** What is Alice's degree?
**Gold:** Alice has a PhD in Neuroscience from UCL (2019).
**Candidate:** Alice earned a doctoral degree in neuroscience at University College London in 2019.

Binary judge: **1** (correct — same factual content)
Token F1:
- tokens(G) = {Alice, has, a, PhD, in, Neuroscience, from, UCL, 2019}  (9 tokens)
- tokens(C) = {Alice, earned, a, doctoral, degree, in, neuroscience, at, University, College, London, in, 2019}  (13 tokens, dedup to 13 unique since "in" appears twice... dedup to 12 unique)
- tokens(G ∩ C) = {Alice, a, in, neuroscience, 2019} (5 tokens, case-insensitive)
- P = 5/12 ≈ 0.42; R = 5/9 ≈ 0.56
- F1 = 2×0.42×0.56 / (0.42+0.56) = **0.48**

**Binary 1.0 vs F1 0.48** — huge gap (52 pp divergence on same semantic correctness).

#### Pair 2 — Verbose answer (binary=1, F1 moderate)

**Question:** Where does Bob work?
**Gold:** Bob works at Google.
**Candidate:** Bob is a senior engineer at Google in Mountain View, California, where he has been employed for 5 years.

Binary judge: **1** (correct — Bob works at Google is contained in response)
Token F1:
- tokens(G) = {Bob, works, at, Google}  (4 tokens)
- tokens(C) = {Bob, is, a, senior, engineer, at, Google, in, Mountain, View, California, where, he, has, been, employed, for, 5, years}  (19 tokens)
- tokens(G ∩ C) = {Bob, at, Google}  (3 tokens)
- P = 3/19 ≈ 0.16; R = 3/4 = 0.75
- F1 = 2×0.16×0.75 / (0.16+0.75) = **0.26**

**Binary 1.0 vs F1 0.26** — extra-information verbose candidates are heavily penalized by F1.

#### Pair 3 — Token-overlap false positive (binary=0, F1 high)

**Question:** When did Alice get her PhD?
**Gold:** Alice got her PhD in 2019.
**Candidate:** Alice got her PhD in 2014.

Binary judge: **0** (wrong year — incorrect)
Token F1:
- tokens(G) = {Alice, got, her, PhD, in, 2019}  (6 tokens)
- tokens(C) = {Alice, got, her, PhD, in, 2014}  (6 tokens)
- tokens(G ∩ C) = {Alice, got, her, PhD, in}  (5 tokens)
- P = 5/6 = 0.83; R = 5/6 = 0.83
- F1 = **0.83**

**Binary 0.0 vs F1 0.83** — F1 gives a high score to a factually WRONG answer that differs in only 1 token.

### 4. Cross-paper comparability implications

The paper's claim in §4.2:
> "Synthius-Mem achieves 94.37% accuracy, exceeding … human performance (87.9%)"

where 94.37% = binary and 87.9% = F1. Under the divergence demonstrated:
- A system with 87.9 F1 could have binary accuracy anywhere from ~70% to ~100% depending on answer verbosity, paraphrasing, and factual precision.
- A system with 94.37% binary accuracy could have F1 anywhere from ~50% to ~95%.

**The comparison is uninterpretable without additional cross-metric calibration.** Exceeding humans on binary does NOT imply exceeding humans on F1; the claim is methodologically unsound.

### 5. Recommended remediation

Either:
- Re-compute Synthius-Mem's F1 score on identical LoCoMo questions → compare F1 vs F1 directly.
- Re-compute human performance under binary judge → compare binary vs binary directly.
- Delete or re-phrase the "exceeding human performance" claim.

Paper's own §5.3 acknowledges the comparability problem but does not apply the remediation in §4.2/abstract.

## Verdict

**PASS for H-119.** The 94.37% binary vs 87.9 F1 comparison is methodologically unsound. The paper's own §5.3 concedes the incomparability. Three synthetic Q-A pairs demonstrate divergences of 52 pp (paraphrase), 74 pp (verbose), and 83 pp (wrong date with high token overlap) — confirming the metrics measure fundamentally different things. Rhetorical "exceeding human performance" claim should be re-framed or retracted.
