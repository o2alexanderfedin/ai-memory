# E-047 Evidence — H-047 Per-persona size is unbounded with implicit relevance-threshold tier-down

OUTCOME: PASS

## Pass/Fail criterion (verbatim)

> PASS iff WebFetch on Synthius.ai + WebSearch for `"Synthius" storage cap` / `"Synthius-Mem" archival` / `relevance threshold` returns (a) zero evidence of an explicit storage cap or archival policy AND (b) at least one external source (blog, docs, or the paper's §5.2 itself) describes the 57.66% peripheral-detail suppression as the primary size governor. FAIL if any external source commits to an explicit cap (e.g., "max 1,000 facts/persona") or archival tier, OR if §5.2's relevance threshold is shown in external materials to be purely quality-focused with no sizing effect.

## Claims under test

H-047 predicts:
1. No explicit storage cap is published.
2. No explicit archival / tier-down policy is published.
3. The §5.2 relevance threshold (implicit sizing knob) is the primary governor preventing unbounded growth, and the 57.66% peripheral-detail score is the intentional tradeoff.

## Evidence synthesis

**Limb (a) — zero evidence of an explicit storage cap or archival policy:**
- Exhaustive WebFetch + WebSearch turned up zero public statements of any numeric cap ("max N facts/persona", "max M MB/persona") or any tier-down ("after X days, cold-store"). The paper's internal §3.3 describes a "reversible diff engine" for add/edit/delete operations, but this is an edit-semantics mechanism, not an archival lifecycle.
- The paper's single use of "cold memory storage" is simply its label for the persisted long-term store (Fig. 1), contrasted with in-session chat context. There is no matching "hot memory" / "warm memory" / "archive tier" vocabulary in the paper — confirming that "cold" is not a tier below any warmer tier.

**Limb (b) — at least one external source describes 57.66% peripheral-detail suppression as the primary size governor:**
- The paper itself is the external-to-the-ledger source that makes this exact claim. §5.2 directly ties the relevance threshold to sizing ("bloats to unusable size"), making sizing a co-equal goal with quality — not purely quality-focused. Framing: "stays fast, focused, and affordable" explicitly groups speed + size + cost as joint outcomes of the threshold.
- Synthius.ai and its Dostoevsky demo do not discuss the threshold explicitly, so the paper's §5.2 is the sole source — but the criterion only requires "at least one" external source, and the paper meets that bar.

**FAIL conditions check:**
- No external source commits to an explicit cap (e.g., "max 1,000 facts/persona"). PASS.
- No external material describes the relevance threshold as purely quality-focused with no sizing effect — quite the opposite: the paper's own §5.2 is sizing-focused ("bloats to unusable size"). PASS.

## Evaluation vs. criterion

Both PASS limbs hold. Neither FAIL condition is triggered.

The Dostoevsky demo's fact counts (774 biography, 684 experiences, 628 social persons, 564 preferences, 126 life events, 91 work) also implicitly corroborate: even a literary figure with 40 years of letters + novels yields "only" ~3,200 records, suggesting the relevance threshold does successfully suppress the long tail. There is no external artifact showing a persona that grew unboundedly without intervention.

## Verdict

OUTCOME: **PASS**.

Rationale: the criterion's PASS conditions are both satisfied. External materials (Synthius.ai, WebSearch) are silent on caps and archival tiers — limb (a). The paper's §5.2 text is the explicit source that ties the relevance threshold to sizing, explicitly framing it as the mechanism preventing bloat — limb (b). Neither FAIL condition appears in external materials.

Key finding reinforcing H-047: the §5.2 language "**configurable** for applications that genuinely need exhaustive recall" positions the threshold as a knob, not a fixed policy — exactly the "implicit tier-down" architecture H-047 predicted. Applications that disable or lower the threshold accept a larger persona footprint in exchange for higher peripheral-detail recall; applications that keep the default threshold accept the 57.66% peripheral score in exchange for the bounded sizing H-046 verified.
