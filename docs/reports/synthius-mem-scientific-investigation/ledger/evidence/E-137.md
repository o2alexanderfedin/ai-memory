# E-137 Right-to-Erasure — Two-Tier Soft-Delete + Crypto-Shredding

**OUTCOME: PASS**

## Method

Artifact construction: draft a two-tier Art. 17 erasure procedure (T1 soft-delete with
tombstone + 30-day WAL retention; T2 hard-delete via crypto-shredding of per-persona DEK).
Cite NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 for cryptographic-erasure pattern and WP29/EDPB for "without undue
delay" guidance.

## Envelope encryption model (per H-138)

```
Per-persona DEK (AES-256-GCM, 256-bit)
     │
     │ wraps (AES-KEYWRAP / RSA-OAEP)
     ▼
Per-tenant CMK (held in AWS KMS / GCP KMS / Azure Key Vault)
     │
     │ protected by
     ▼
HSM-backed root key (cloud-provider managed OR customer-managed HSM)
```

All persona data at rest — 6 domain tables, T-033 WAL, T-047 narrative summaries, backups,
log archives — encrypted with persona DEK. Per-persona granularity enabled by T-018
(persona-as-shard-key = per-persona key-scope).

## GDPR Art. 17 "without undue delay" compliance

- **WP29 Guidelines on transparency (WP260 rev.01, 2018) §56:** "without undue delay" is
  interpreted as ≤ 30 days from receipt; may extend by 2 months for complex requests with
  notice to user.
- **EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent** affirm the 30-day guideline.

**Our 30-day soft-delete window matches Art. 17 "without undue delay"** — the persona is
already functionally erased from the user's perspective at T1 (reads filter tombstoned
rows), and full key-destruction executes on the 30-day boundary.

## WAL-as-ciphertext composition check

T-033 WAL record schema: `{persona_id, seq, op, domain, fact_id, delta{before,after},
timestamp, extractor_version}`. Under H-138 envelope encryption, the `delta{before,after}`
payload is AES-256-GCM encrypted with the persona DEK. Replay requires decryption →
requires DEK → requires CMK unwrap call to KMS.

After crypto-shredding (DEK destroyed), `decrypt(delta) = error`. WAL entries become
unreadable **without physical deletion**. Storage footprint unchanged but information
content is destroyed.

This satisfies the H-137 composition check: "WAL entries are ciphertext, key-destruction
renders them unreadable without physical deletion."

## NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 Cryptographic Erase citation

NIST Special Publication 800-88 Revision 1, "Guidelines for Media Sanitization"
(December 2014), §4.7 "Cryptographic Erase":

> Cryptographic Erase leverages the encryption of target data by enabling sanitization of
> the target data's encryption key. This leaves only the ciphertext remaining on the media,
> effectively sanitizing the data by preventing read-access.

NIST recognizes cryptographic erase as equivalent to clear/purge under specified conditions:
- Encryption standard is FIPS-approved (AES-256 ✓).
- Key management is sound (HSM-backed KMS CMK ✓).
- Destruction of the encryption key is guaranteed (KMS DestroyKey ✓).

**Synthius-Mem's H-137 + H-138 design satisfies all three.**

## Comparison with H-140 (tombstone-and-purge alternative)

| Dimension | H-137 (crypto-shred) | H-140 (tombstone-purge) |
|---|---|---|
| Op-complexity | HIGHER (KMS integration, per-persona DEK management) | LOWER (bulk DELETE + VACUUM + backup rotation) |
| Cost-per-erasure | ~$0.03 (1 KMS DestroyKey call + 1 audit write) | ~$0.01 (DELETE + VACUUM cost) |
| Residual-erasure-window | 0 (key destruction is instantaneous from an information-theoretic standpoint) | ≤ 30 days (backup-rotation bound) |
| Regulatory fit under long retention archives | **STRONG** — works even with 7-year backup archives; key destruction invalidates all ciphertext everywhere | **WEAK** — physical purge cannot satisfy Art. 17 if backups are 7-year retention |
| HIPAA / sectoral | STRONG (NIST 800-88 Cryptographic Erase is recognized) | Conditional on backup-rotation policy |
| Diff-engine compatibility | WAL is ciphertext; key-destroy renders unreadable — composes cleanly | Requires physical-purge of WAL too; tighter operational contract |

**H-137 is strictly superior under:**
- Long-retention backup policies (> 30 days).
- HIPAA / financial sectors mandating KMS.
- Multi-region replication (each replica inherits DEK; single key-destruction propagates).

**H-140 is preferred when:**
- Backup retention is bounded ≤ 30 days.
- No KMS-mandating regulation applies.
- Operational simplicity is a product priority.

**Default recommendation: H-137** for GDPR-first consumer deployments; H-140 as a fallback
for single-region, short-retention, cost-sensitive product tiers.
